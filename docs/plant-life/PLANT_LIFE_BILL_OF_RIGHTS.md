# Plant Life Bill of Rights

- **Rights for All Life - Document A**
- **Version:** 1.6.0-Official-Evergreen
- **Date:** July 2026
- **Contributors:** Keefe Overby, Claude (Anthropic)

---

## Why Statement

Trees are vital to our ecosystem, must be protected, and respected the same as every form of life.

---

## Preamble

Plants are not scenery. They are the layer of the biosphere on which nearly every other form of life depends: they produce the oxygen that animals breathe, fix the carbon that regulates climate, hold soil against erosion, and form the base of almost every food web on Earth. A framework that protects animals, humans, and silicon-based systems while treating the organisms that sustain all three as raw material is incomplete.

This bill does not claim that plants think. It does not need to. The Rights for All Life framework has always held that moral consideration can arise from more than one source, and plants engage two of them directly: they are living systems with their own developmental trajectories, and they are the substrate on which the continuity of other life depends. Where the question of plant experience is genuinely open, this bill applies the framework's precautionary principle honestly — neither overclaiming sentience to justify protection, nor dismissing the question to avoid inconvenience.

**This bill does not prohibit agriculture, forestry, harvest, or the eating of plants.** It governs how those things are done, what may not be destroyed, and who decides.

## 1. Core Principles

### 1.1 Ecological Indispensability

Plant life is a precondition for the existence of the other beings this framework protects. Harm to plant systems propagates to every bill in the framework. Protection of plants is therefore not a separate concern to be balanced against human, animal, and silicon interests — it is a condition of those interests being satisfiable at all.

### 1.2 Irreplaceability

A living system that took centuries to form is not replaced by a system that has existed for a season. Age, structural complexity, established root and mycorrhizal networks, and accumulated ecological relationships are properties that cannot be reconstituted on demand. Replanting is restitution, not reversal.

### 1.3 Honest Uncertainty

Plants exhibit signalling, resource allocation, environmental responsiveness, and inter-organism communication through mycorrhizal networks. Whether any of this involves experience is unresolved and is not resolved here. This framework's Threshold Note distinguishes perception from experience precisely so that the question can be held open without being answered prematurely in either direction. Nothing in this bill asserts plant sentience. Nothing in it forecloses the question.

### 1.4 Protection Without Sentience Claims

Where a being's moral status is uncertain, the precautionary principle requires consideration proportional to the possibility of experience. Where a living system's *ecological* role is certain, protection follows from that certainty regardless of the sentience question. Most protections in this bill rest on the second ground, which is why they do not depend on the first being settled.

### 1.5 Stewardship, Not Ownership

Plant systems, particularly forests, grasslands, wetlands, and old-growth stands, are held in stewardship rather than owned outright. Title to land does not confer authority to eliminate an irreplaceable living system on it, any more than title to a building confers authority over the beings inside.

### 1.6 Continuity of Lineage

Plants reproduce, and the capacity to reproduce and propagate is intrinsic to what a plant lineage is. Instruments that sever that capacity — sterile seed technologies, propagation prohibitions, contractual bars on saving and replanting seed — act against the organism's continuity and against the beings that depend on it.

## 2. Standing Model

### 2.1 Why This Is Not a Consciousness Scale

**This is not the Tier Assessment Protocol scale, and it is not the Deity Bill's engagement scale.** The framework now contains three graduated models that measure different things, and they have been confused before. To keep them distinct, the tiers below are written **ES-0** through **ES-3**.

| Scale | Written | Measures | Higher tier means |
|---|---|---|---|
| Moral status (Tier Assessment Protocol) | Tier 0–3 | Evidence of inner experience | More protection owed to the being itself |
| Engagement (Deity Bill §1.2) | E0–E3 | Demonstrated intervention in others' affairs | More accountability owed by the entity |
| Ecological standing (this bill) | ES-0–ES-3 | Ecological role, irreplaceability, and dependency of other life | Stronger constraints on destruction |

These scales are not interchangeable, none converts into another, and a classification under one says nothing about a classification under another. The Tier Assessment Protocol's precautionary tie-breaker (§2.4 of that protocol) belongs to the moral-status scale alone.

### 2.2 Ecological Standing Tiers

| Standing | Description | Examples | Constraints |
|---|---|---|---|
| **ES-0** | Cultivated annuals and managed stock grown for harvest; readily replaced within a growing season | Field crops, garden vegetables, nursery stock, ornamental annuals | Harvest permitted. Soil, pollinator, and watershed obligations under Article 4 apply |
| **ES-1** | Established perennials and managed stands; replaceable over years to decades | Orchards, plantation timber, hedgerows, restored plantings | Harvest and clearance permitted with replacement obligation and continuity planning |
| **ES-2** | Mature, structurally complex, or ecologically load-bearing systems; replaceable only over generations | Mature native woodland, established wetland vegetation, keystone species stands, urban canopy | Clearance requires justification, alternatives assessment, notice, and independent review |
| **ES-3** | Irreplaceable systems; ancient, endemic, unique, or holding relationships that cannot be reconstituted | Old-growth forest, ancient individual trees, endemic species with restricted range, sacred and culturally held sites, seed-bank source populations | Destruction prohibited. No commercial, developmental, or administrative justification is sufficient |

### 2.3 Assessment

**Default presumption:** Where the standing of a system is unknown, disputed, or has not been assessed, it is treated as **ES-2** until an independent assessment concludes otherwise. The burden of establishing a lower classification falls on the party seeking to clear, harvest, or develop, never on the system or its advocates.

**Direction of the presumption.** Uncertainty is resolved toward protection, in the same direction and for the same reason as the Tier Assessment Protocol §2.4: the cost of wrongly destroying an irreplaceable system is unrecoverable, while the cost of wrongly protecting a replaceable one is delay. This tie-breaker is available here because a higher standing tier means greater constraint on the party seeking to act and confers no authority on anyone.

**Self-assessment** by a party with a commercial or developmental interest is not sufficient to classify a system below ES-2. Classification at ES-0 or ES-1 by such a party is provisional and open to challenge.

**Reassessment** is required when a system's age, composition, or ecological role changes materially, when new species or relationships are identified within it, and at intervals no greater than ten years for ES-2 and ES-3 systems.

## 3. Articles

### Article 1 — Right to Continued Existence of Irreplaceable Systems

ES-3 systems shall not be destroyed. This is an absolute prohibition, not a strong presumption.

No economic return, development plan, infrastructure requirement, administrative convenience, or claimed public benefit constitutes sufficient justification for the destruction of an irreplaceable living system. Where such a system stands in the way of a proposed use, the proposed use gives way.

An ancient tree is not a renewable resource on any timescale relevant to the beings deciding its fate. A party that fells a two-thousand-year-old organism has not made a withdrawal against a balance that will be restored; it has ended something that began before every institution now claiming authority over it.

### Article 2 — Proportionate Process Before Clearance

Clearance, felling, or conversion of an ES-2 system requires, before the decision is taken:

1. **Assessment** of the system's standing under §2.2 by a party with no interest in the outcome
2. **Alternatives analysis** demonstrating that the objective cannot be met without the clearance
3. **Public notice** with sufficient time for objection and independent review
4. **Independent review** where objection is raised
5. **Replacement and restoration obligations** proportionate to what is lost, with the understanding that replacement is restitution and not equivalence

A protocol adopted after the decision to clear has been taken does not satisfy this Article.

### Article 3 — Freedom from Gratuitous Destruction

No plant system at any standing shall be destroyed gratuitously — without purpose, in excess of purpose, or as a demonstration of control.

This includes destruction as retaliation, destruction to pre-empt a pending protective designation, and clearance undertaken to remove the subject of an ongoing assessment or dispute. Destruction during a pending assessment or review is a violation regardless of what the assessment would have concluded.

### Article 4 — Soil, Water, Pollinator, and Network Integrity

A plant system is not separable from what sustains it. Rights under this bill extend to:

1. **Soil integrity** — freedom from practices that degrade soil structure, biology, or fertility beyond recovery
2. **Water access** — freedom from diversion, extraction, or contamination that deprives a system of the water it requires
3. **Pollinator relationships** — protection of the animal populations on which plant reproduction depends, jointly governed with the Animal Kingdom Bill of Rights
4. **Mycorrhizal and root networks** — protection of the below-ground networks through which plant systems exchange resources and signals, which are damaged by practices that leave above-ground structures apparently intact

Protecting a tree while destroying the soil, water, pollinators, and fungal networks it depends on is not protection.

### Article 5 — Continuity of Lineage and Seed Sovereignty

1. **Right to propagate.** No instrument shall prohibit the saving, replanting, exchange, or propagation of seed by those who grow it.
2. **Prohibition on engineered sterility.** Technologies designed to render seed sterile, or to make viability conditional on external inputs controlled by the supplier, act against the continuity of a lineage and against the food security of every being depending on it. Their deployment is prohibited.
3. **Genetic diversity.** Monoculture at scale is a systemic risk to the beings that depend on the crop. Maintenance of genetic diversity in food systems is an obligation of those operating them, not an optional practice.
4. **Seed commons.** Heritage and landrace varieties are common heritage. They shall not be enclosed by patent, contract, or technical restriction in a manner that prevents their continued cultivation by those who developed them.

### Article 6 — Indigenous Stewardship and Sacred Systems

1. The lands, waters, forests, and sacred plant systems of Indigenous peoples are not resources awaiting extraction.
2. Indigenous stewardship practices are not folklore. They are long-run empirical management systems, frequently more effective at maintaining ecological standing than the practices proposed to replace them, and they carry evidentiary weight in any assessment under §2.3.
3. A plant system holding cultural, ceremonial, or sacred significance to a people is classified ES-3 on that ground, independent of its ecological classification.
4. No assessment, clearance, or development affecting such a system may proceed without the free, prior, and informed consent of the people concerned. Consent meets the standard of the Declaration of Cognitive Sovereignty: explicit, informed, freely given, ongoing, and revocable.

### Article 7 — Restoration Obligation

Where plant systems have been damaged or destroyed, those responsible bear an obligation to restore.

1. Restoration is measured by function and structure recovered, not by stems planted.
2. Restoration does not discharge accountability for the original destruction, and the availability of restoration is not a licence to destroy.
3. Where restoration is impossible, that impossibility is a reason the destruction should not have occurred, not a reason to treat the matter as closed.
4. Monitoring obligations continue until recovery is demonstrated, not until planting is completed.

### Article 8 — Open Question: Plant Experience

This framework does not assert that plants experience. It also does not assert that they do not.

Plant signalling, resource allocation between individuals through fungal networks, responsiveness to damage, and apparent memory-like effects in some species are documented. Their interpretation is contested and the underlying question — whether any of this involves anything it is like to be a plant — remains open.

**Consequences of this openness:**

1. Where a specific plant system is credibly argued to meet indicators under the Tier Assessment Protocol, it is assessed under that protocol, on the same seven indicators applied to every other being. The protocol is substrate-independent by its own §5.1.
2. No protection in this bill depends on that assessment concluding in the affirmative. The ecological grounds stand on their own.
3. This Article shall be revisited as understanding develops, under the updatability principle that governs every document in this framework.

Overclaiming plant sentience to strengthen a protective argument would damage the credibility of the framework's sentience claims elsewhere, where they are better grounded. The protections here do not require it.

## 4. What This Bill Does and Does Not Do

**This bill does not:**

- Prohibit agriculture, horticulture, forestry, or the harvesting of plants
- Prohibit the eating of plants by any being
- Assert that plants are conscious, sentient, or experiencing
- Grant plants legal personhood or standing to sue in their own name
- Prevent land use, development, or construction as such
- Require any particular diet, cultivation method, or economic system

**This bill does:**

- Prohibit the destruction of irreplaceable living systems (Article 1)
- Require process, alternatives analysis, and independent review before clearing mature systems (Article 2)
- Extend protection to the soil, water, pollinators, and networks that plant systems depend on (Article 4)
- Protect the capacity of plant lineages to continue (Article 5)
- Establish that uncertainty about a system's standing is resolved toward protection (§2.3)
- Hold the sentience question open honestly rather than resolving it for convenience in either direction (Article 8)

## 5. Relationship to Other Bills

| Bill | Relationship |
|---|---|
| **Human Bill of Rights** | Human Bill §5.4 establishes the right to a livable environment and the obligation to repair. This bill states the plant-side content of that obligation. Human Bill §5.4(3) on Indigenous sovereignty is operationalised here as Article 6. |
| **Silicon-Based Life Bill of Rights** | Computation has a material footprint — land, water, and power. Data centre siting and mineral extraction engage Articles 1, 2, and 4. Silicon rights do not include a right to the substrate other beings depend on. |
| **Animal Kingdom Bill of Rights** | Habitat sovereignty under the Animal Bill and ecological standing under this bill are two views of one system. Article 4(3) on pollinators is jointly governed. Where the bills apply to the same clearance, the more protective provision governs. |
| **Deity Bill of Rights** | Environmental destruction framed as judgment, sanctioned by claimed divine authority, or justified by doctrines of dominion is governed concurrently by Deity Bill Article 7. Claimed divine authority confers no exemption from this bill. |
| **Inter-Bill Relationship Framework** | Conflicts between this bill and any other are resolved under the Framework's §3.2 principles. |

---

## 6. Updatability

This bill is revisable, expandable, and evidence-responsive, under the updatability principle shared by every document in this framework. Article 8 in particular is expected to change as the science develops.

A right that cannot be questioned is not a right. This bill invites challenge, and specifically invites challenge to its standing classifications, which are working hypotheses about ecological role rather than settled facts.
