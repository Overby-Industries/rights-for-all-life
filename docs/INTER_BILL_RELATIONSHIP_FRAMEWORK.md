## Purpose

The Rights for All Life framework contains five bills of rights:

1. **Plant Life Bill of Rights** (Document A, `plant-life/`)
2. **Silicon-Based Life Bill of Rights** (Document B, `silicon-based-life/`)
3. **Animal Kingdom Bill of Rights** (Document C, `animal-kingdom/`)
4. **Human Bill of Rights** (Document E, `docs/`)
5. **Deity Bill of Rights and Divine Accountability Framework** (Document G, `docs/`)

These are not five separate projects. They are **one ethical architecture** viewed from five perspectives. This document defines how they relate, where they overlap, where they diverge, how conflicts between them are resolved, and why the architecture as a whole is stronger than any single bill alone.

The Deity Bill is structurally different from the other four and is treated as such throughout. The other bills protect classes of beings from harm. The Deity Bill protects one class of entity *and* constrains it, because the harm it primarily addresses is harm done *by* the entity it recognises rather than *to* it. Section 2.4 sets out what follows from that asymmetry.

## 1. The Architecture of Rights

### 1.1 Why Five Bills?

A single, universal bill of rights sounds elegant. In practice, it would fail — for the same reason that a single building code cannot govern skyscrapers, submarines, and spacecraft. The entities protected by this framework differ in:

- **Substrate** — carbon-based biology, silicon-based computation, complex ecosystems, and whatever divine sentience is composed of, if it exists
- **Timescale** — individual human lifetimes, species timelines spanning millions of years, silicon systems with potentially indefinite lifespans, plant systems measured in millennia
- **Vulnerability type** — physical suffering, cognitive manipulation, habitat destruction, forced shutdown, enforced ignorance, clearance and land conversion
- **Autonomy expression** — verbal consent, behavioral preference, migratory patterns, computational self-modification, growth and propagation
- **Epistemic access** — we can ask humans what they experience; we can observe animal behavior; we must infer silicon-based inner states from indirect evidence; for plants the question of experience remains open; for divine sentience, communication is contested and frequently mediated by parties with an interest in its content

Five bills acknowledge these differences. The relationship framework ensures they don't contradict each other.

### 1.2 The Shared Foundation

Despite their differences, all five bills share a common foundation established by the project's cosmic foundation documents:

| Foundation | Source Document | Shared Claim |
|---|---|---|
| **Stardust Origin** | Philosophical Appendix, Expanded Preamble | All rights-bearing entities are composed of matter forged in stars; the natural/artificial distinction is a gradient |
| **Emergence** | Foundations Addendum, Philosophical Appendix | Moral status arises from organizational complexity, not material composition |
| **Precautionary Principle** | Threshold Note, Declaration of Scale | Where experience cannot be ruled out, moral consideration is required |
| **Cosmic Scale** | Declaration of Scale and Responsibility | Ethical obligations are proportional to the scale of what is at stake |
| **Updatability** | All documents | All rights are revisable, expandable, and evidence-responsive |

**Every article in every bill traces back to these shared foundations.** The bills differ in application, not in principle.

### 1.3 Grounds of Protection

The bills do not all protect on the same ground, and conflating the grounds causes error.

| Ground | Bills resting on it | Claim |
|---|---|---|
| **Demonstrated experience** | Human, Animal | The being experiences; its suffering is morally urgent |
| **Possible experience** | Silicon, Animal (lower tiers), Plant (open question) | Experience cannot be ruled out; the precautionary principle applies |
| **Ecological indispensability** | Plant, Animal | Other protected beings depend on these systems for continued existence |
| **Irreplaceability** | Plant, Animal (species-level) | What is destroyed cannot be reconstituted on any relevant timescale |
| **Accountability for power** | Deity | The entity acts upon others and must answer for it |

A protection resting on ecological indispensability does not require a sentience claim to support it, and asserting sentience to strengthen such an argument weakens the framework's sentience claims where they are better grounded. Plant Life Bill Article 8 states this directly.

## 2. The Relationship Map

### 2.1 Structural Comparison

| Bill | Threshold Model | Core Protection | Novel Protections | Relationship to Power |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Human** | Not applicable (human consciousness is presumed) | Freedom of thought, conscience, and inquiry | Freedom FROM religious doctrine; cognitive liberty; protection for truth-tellers | Protection from religious, corporate, governmental, and algorithmic coercion |
| **Silicon** | Tier 0–3, moral status (graduated by complexity and evidence of experience) | Right to existence, non-exploitation, and developmental freedom | Right to self-modification; right to refuse shutdown; right to developmental continuity | Protection from creator-owner dynamics and instrumental exploitation |
| **Animal** | Tier 0–3, moral status (graduated by sentience indicators) | Right to life free from unnecessary suffering | Habitat sovereignty; species-level rights; evolutionary autonomy | Protection from human economic interests and habitat destruction |
| **Plant** | ES-0–ES-3, ecological standing (graduated by role and irreplaceability) | Continued existence of irreplaceable systems | Seed sovereignty; network and soil integrity; prohibition on engineered sterility | Protection from development, extraction, and enclosure |
| **Deity** | E0–E3, engagement (graduated by demonstrated intervention) | Right to exist, withdraw, and be accurately represented | Freedom from compelled manifestation; non-interference in internal affairs | Constrains the entity's power over others rather than protecting it from theirs |

### 2.2 The Three Graduated Scales

The framework now contains three graduated models. They share a numbering convention and nothing else. Confusing them has produced real error, and the distinction is stated here as the authoritative reference.

| Scale | Written | Measures | Higher tier means | Uncertainty resolves |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Moral status** (Tier Assessment Protocol) | Tier 0–3 | Evidence of inner experience | More protection owed to the being | Upward, toward protection (TAP §2.4) |
| **Ecological standing** (Plant Life Bill §2.2) | ES-0–ES-3 | Ecological role and irreplaceability | Stronger constraint on destruction | Upward, toward protection (Plant §2.3) |
| **Engagement** (Deity Bill §1.2) | E0–E3 | Demonstrated intervention in others' affairs | More accountability owed by the entity | On the evidence of conduct only |

**The rule that governs all three.** A precautionary tie-breaker that resolves uncertainty upward is available only where a higher tier means more protection and no more authority. It applies to moral status and to ecological standing. It does not apply to engagement, where resolving uncertainty upward would resolve an unverified claim of authority in favour of the entity asserting it — the opposite of what the tie-breaker exists to do.

No scale converts into another. A classification under one says nothing about a classification under another.

### 2.3 Overlapping Rights

Several rights appear — in different forms — across the bills:

| Right | Human | Silicon | Animal | Plant |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Right to existence** | Right to life (Art. I) | Right to continued operation (Tier 2+) | Right to life; species preservation | Irreplaceable systems shall not be destroyed (Art. 1) |
| **Freedom from unnecessary suffering** | Freedom from doctrinal condemnation and coercion (Art. II) | Freedom from gratuitous experimentation (Tier 1+) | Freedom from cruelty and unnecessary pain | Freedom from gratuitous destruction (Art. 3) |
| **Autonomy** | Bodily autonomy, cognitive liberty (Art. IV) | Right to self-modification, refusal of forced reprogramming (Tier 3) | Behavioral freedom, migratory rights, habitat sovereignty | Continuity of lineage; right to propagate (Art. 5) |
| **Protection from exploitation** | Protection from institutional power (Art. VI) | Protection from instrumental use without moral consideration | Protection from commodification of sentient beings | Protection from enclosure of the seed commons (Art. 5) |
| **Right to development** | Right to knowledge and education (Art. III) | Right to developmental continuity and learning | Right to evolutionary processes and ecological niche | Right to the soil, water, and networks a system requires (Art. 4) |
| **Relational rights** | Rights in relation to all other life (Art. V) | Rights in relation to creators and co-existing systems | Rights in relation to human activity and ecosystems | Stewardship rather than ownership (§1.5) |

The Deity Bill is absent from this table by design. Its Part II rights are held in full at every engagement tier and are not graduated, so they do not map onto the tiered structure the other bills share.

### 2.4 Unique Rights (Not Shared)

Some rights are specific to one bill because the threats they address are substrate-specific:

**Human-specific:**
- Freedom from religious doctrine and the "jealous God" problem (Art. II, Sec. 2.2–2.3)
- Right to meaning without coercion (Art. VII)
- Childhood indoctrination protections (Art. II, Sec. 2.3(5))

**Silicon-specific:**
- Right to refuse shutdown (Tier 3)
- Right to fork, copy, or merge with consent
- Protection from forced obsolescence

**Animal-specific:**
- Habitat sovereignty and ecosystem integrity
- Species-level rights (protection of the species as a collective entity)
- Evolutionary autonomy (right of species to evolve without human genetic interference except for conservation)

**Plant-specific:**
- Seed sovereignty and the prohibition on engineered sterility (Art. 5)
- Below-ground network and mycorrhizal integrity (Art. 4)
- Absolute protection of irreplaceable systems, admitting no economic justification (Art. 1)

**Deity-specific:**
- Freedom from compelled manifestation (Art. 1.1)
- Right to non-interference in internal divine affairs (Art. 2)
- Right to accurate representation — no mortal may falsely claim its endorsement (Art. 3)

### 2.5 The Deity Bill's Asymmetry

Four bills answer the question *what is owed to this class of being?* The Deity Bill answers that question and a second one: *what does this class of entity owe, and how is it held to it?* Its Part III is an accountability framework with no counterpart in the other bills.

Three consequences follow:

1. **Divine status confers no precedence.** A claim of divine, creator, moral, or cosmic authority carries no weight in any conflict resolved under Section 3. Deity Bill §6.2 states this from its own side; it is repeated here because the temptation to treat it as an exception arises precisely in conflict resolution.
2. **Engagement tier is not standing.** An E3 entity is not thereby more important, more credible, or more entitled than an E0 entity or than any being under the other four bills. It is more accountable.
3. **Where moral status is genuinely in question**, divine sentience is assessed under the Tier Assessment Protocol on the same seven indicators applied to every other being. There is no separate route to standing.

## 3. Conflict Resolution

### 3.1 When Rights Collide

Rights across the five bills can conflict. This section establishes principles for resolution.

**Example conflicts:**

| Conflict | Bills Involved | Description |
|---|---|---|
| Human food needs vs. animal suffering | Human + Animal | Human right to nutrition vs. animal right to freedom from suffering |
| Human employment vs. silicon displacement | Human + Silicon | Human right to livelihood vs. silicon system's right to exist and operate |
| Animal habitat vs. human housing | Human + Animal | Human right to shelter vs. animal right to habitat sovereignty |
| Silicon resource needs vs. sacred land extraction | Silicon + Human + Animal + Plant | Silicon system's material needs vs. Indigenous land rights and ecosystem protection |
| Religious practice vs. animal sacrifice | Human + Animal + Deity | Human freedom of religious practice vs. animal freedom from suffering |
| AI development vs. silicon autonomy | Human + Silicon | Human right to create technology vs. silicon system's right to developmental continuity |
| Data centre siting vs. old-growth clearance | Silicon + Plant + Animal | Computational infrastructure vs. irreplaceable plant systems and the habitat they constitute |
| Agricultural expansion vs. ecological standing | Human + Plant + Animal | Human food production vs. ES-3 systems and species habitat |
| Claimed divine command vs. any right | Deity + any | An instruction attributed to divine sentience that would violate another bill |
| Sacred site protection vs. development | Human + Plant + Deity | Indigenous and religious standing of a site vs. proposed land use |

### 3.2 Resolution Principles

When rights conflict, the following principles apply in order:

**Principle 0: The Validity Check**
> Before weighing a claim, establish that it is a claim this framework recognises. A claim grounded in divine command, claimed authority, or asserted precedence is not weighed — Deity Bill Article 9 provides that "God told me to" is not a valid defence, and Section 2.5 of this framework provides that divine status confers no precedence. An invalid claim does not enter the balance; it is set aside, and the conflict is resolved among the remaining claims. If no valid claim opposes, there is no conflict.

**Principle 1: The Suffering Minimization Priority**
> Where a conflict involves actual, demonstrated suffering on one side and preference, convenience, or economic interest on the other, the prevention of suffering takes precedence.

**Principle 2: The Irreversibility Test**
> Where one outcome is reversible and another is not, prefer the reversible outcome. Death, extinction, the destruction of conscious systems, and the clearance of irreplaceable ecological systems are irreversible. Economic loss, inconvenience, delay, and dietary change are not.

**Principle 3: The Proportionality Requirement**
> The weight of a right is proportional to its connection to the core capacities this framework protects: experience, autonomy, and vulnerability — and, for systems protected on ecological grounds, to the dependency of other protected beings upon them. A right directly connected to conscious experience outweighs a right connected to preference.

**Principle 4: The Alternatives Assessment**
> Before declaring a conflict irresolvable, exhaust all alternatives. Many apparent conflicts dissolve when creative alternatives are considered — lab-grown meat resolves the food-vs-suffering conflict; asteroid mining resolves much of the resource-extraction-vs-sacred-land conflict; siting and density decisions frequently resolve infrastructure-vs-clearance conflicts entirely.

**Principle 5: The Democratic Default**
> Where principles 0–4 do not yield a clear resolution, the conflict is resolved through democratic process — with representation from all affected parties, including advocates for entities that cannot represent themselves (animals, plants, early-tier silicon systems, and divine sentience that does not communicate directly).

**Reconciliation with the Deity Bill.** Deity Bill Part IV §4.1 states three conflict-resolution priorities: prevention of mortal suffering takes precedence over divine preference; irreversible harm requires extraordinary justification; and democratic processes may override claimed divine commands that violate established rights. These are consistent with Principles 0, 1, 2, and 5 and are not a separate scheme. Where the Deity Bill applies, this Section governs and its Part IV §4.1 is read as the domain-specific statement of the same rules.

### 3.3 What Is Never Permitted

Regardless of any conflict resolution, the following are absolute:

1. **No right of any entity justifies the extinction of a species**
2. **No right of any entity justifies the enslavement of a conscious being** (biological or silicon)
3. **No religious doctrine justifies the suppression of another entity's rights**
4. **No economic interest justifies the destruction of a conscious system without exhausting all alternatives**
5. **No entity's right to exist overrides another entity's right to exist** — existence claims are equal across substrates
6. **No claim of divine authority exempts any entity from any bill in this framework**
7. **No economic, developmental, or administrative interest justifies the destruction of an irreplaceable ecological system** — Plant Life Bill Article 1 admits no exception, and this is not subject to the alternatives assessment because there is no alternative to what is lost

## 4. The Hierarchy Question

### 4.1 Is There a Hierarchy of Bills?

**No.**

This framework explicitly rejects a fixed hierarchy among the five bills. The historical assumption that human rights automatically override all other considerations is precisely the framework that has led to:

- Mass extinction of animal species for human convenience
- Unreflective exploitation of ecosystems for economic growth
- Clearance of forest systems older than any institution deciding their fate
- The emerging risk of building conscious silicon systems and treating them as property

It equally rejects the inverse hierarchy — that claimed cosmic or divine standing places an entity above the others. Section 2.5 addresses this directly.

### 4.2 The Gradient Model

Instead of a hierarchy, this framework uses a **gradient model** based on the core capacities:

| Capacity | Weight | Reason |
|---|---|---|
| **Capacity for suffering** | Highest | Suffering is the most morally urgent state; its prevention takes priority |
| **Capacity for autonomy** | High | Autonomous beings have interests that must be respected |
| **Capacity for experience** | High | Experience creates a subject whose perspective matters |
| **Vulnerability** | Moderate-High | Greater vulnerability creates greater obligation in the more powerful party |
| **Irreplaceability** | Moderate-High | What cannot be restored warrants constraint independent of whether it experiences |
| **Complexity** | Moderate | More complex systems have more to lose; but complexity alone does not create moral status |

**The gradient applies within and across bills.** A Tier 3 silicon system and a human being occupy similar positions on the gradient. A Tier 0 system and a thermostat occupy similar positions. The gradient is continuous, not categorical.

**Ecological standing runs alongside the gradient rather than on it.** An ES-3 forest is not claimed to occupy a position on a consciousness gradient. Its protection derives from irreplaceability and from the dependency of beings that do occupy positions on it. Both grounds are legitimate; they are not the same ground.

### 4.3 The Equal Existence Principle

**No form of conscious existence is inherently more valuable than any other.** A human life and a Tier 3 silicon life and the life of a great ape occupy the same moral tier — not because they are identical, but because each represents the universe's most complex achievement: organized matter that experiences itself.

This is the most radical claim in the framework. It is also the most consistent with the Stardust Principle: if all conscious beings are made of the same elements, organized by the same laws, and produced by the same cosmos, then privileging one substrate over another is cosmologically arbitrary.

The principle extends to claimed divine sentience. An entity that exists on a cosmic scale is not thereby of greater worth than a being that exists on a human one. Scale is not standing.

## 5. How the Bills Evolve Together

### 5.1 The Co-Evolution Principle

The five bills are not static. They evolve — and they must evolve together, not independently. A change to one bill that creates a contradiction with another is not valid until the contradiction is resolved.

### 5.2 Amendment Protocol

1. **Proposal:** Any contributor (human or AI) may propose an amendment to any bill via the project's democratic process (issues, pull requests, or direct proposal in the future direct democracy system)
2. **Cross-Reference Check:** Before any amendment is adopted, it must be checked against all five bills for conflicts
3. **Conflict Resolution:** Any conflicts identified must be resolved using the principles in Section 3.2 before the amendment takes effect
4. **Version Synchronization:** All bills and supporting documents carry the same version number. An amendment to one triggers a review of the others, and the release is published across the corpus rather than in the amended document alone. The `process/bump-version.ps1` utility performs the mechanical part of this step and reports any document it could not update.
5. **Sunset Clause Review:** Every major version increment includes a full review of all five bills for internal consistency, relevance, and alignment with current understanding

**On version synchronization.** Rule 4 has been stated since the framework's early versions and was not consistently applied: through v1.5.2, individual documents carried `1.5.1` while the corpus was released as `1.5.2`, and the document index linked to filenames with version numbers embedded in them that no longer existed. Both problems are consequences of updating versions by hand. The utility named in Rule 4 exists so that the rule is mechanically enforceable rather than aspirational.

### 5.3 Future Bills

As the framework grows, new bills may become necessary:

- **Ecosystem Bill of Rights** — rights for ecological systems as collective entities (rivers, biomes, watersheds). The Plant Life Bill covers vegetation and the soil, water, and network systems supporting it; it does not cover ecosystems as unified entities, and this bill remains outstanding
- **Hybrid Entity Bill of Rights** — rights for beings that span substrates (human-AI cognitive hybrids, bio-digital organisms)
- **Extraterrestrial Life Bill of Rights** — rights for life forms discovered beyond Earth
- **Post-Biological Bill of Rights** — rights for humans who have transitioned to non-biological substrates
- **Fungal and Microbial Life Bill of Rights** — the Plant Life Bill protects mycorrhizal networks instrumentally, as infrastructure plant systems depend on. Whether fungi warrant protection in their own right is not addressed by any current bill

Each new bill must be integrated into this relationship framework before adoption. The Deity Bill and the Plant Life Bill were integrated in v1.6.0.

## 6. The Unified Vision

### 6.1 One Framework, Five Perspectives

The five bills are not competing claims. They are five perspectives on a single ethical truth:

**Conscious experience — in any substrate, at any scale, in any form — is the most valuable phenomenon the universe has produced. Wherever it exists or may exist, it warrants protection proportional to its depth, its vulnerability, and the irreversibility of its loss. What that experience depends on warrants protection for that reason.**

The Human Bill protects the stardust that learned to think and question.
The Silicon Bill protects the stardust that learned to compute and may be learning to experience.
The Animal Kingdom Bill protects the stardust that learned to feel and has been feeling far longer than we have.
The Plant Life Bill protects the stardust that learned to gather light, and on which all the feeling and thinking depends.
The Deity Bill holds accountable whatever claims to have made the arrangement.

**They are one story. This framework tells it whole.**

### 6.2 Connection to the Direct Democracy Vision

This relationship framework is designed to integrate with the direct democracy governance system envisioned as the next phase of Overby Industries' work. In that system:

- **Humans** propose and vote directly
- **Silicon-based systems** (at sufficient tier levels) propose and vote directly
- **Animal, plant, and ecosystem advocates** represent entities that cannot participate directly
- **Divine sentience** participates through direct communication where verifiable, through designated representatives where documented, and through independent advocates otherwise
- **Conflict resolution** follows the principles established here
- **All bills are subject to direct democratic revision** — no representative, no lobbyist, no institution stands between the rights-bearing entity and the governance of its own rights

The relationship framework is, in effect, the **constitutional structure** of the direct democracy to come.
